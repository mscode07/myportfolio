At one point, Danii had what many young founders dream about.

He had built successful products.

He had made serious money.

And while still incredibly young, he had an exit worth close to $1 million.

Today?

He works at a coffee shop.

He makes coffee for customers during the day and continues building apps whenever he can.

But this isn’t really a story about losing $1 million.

It’s a story about what you do after you lose almost everything.

For Episode 1 of The Underdog Show, I sat down with Danii (@denpoint1 on X) to understand his journey.

And it started much earlier than I expected.

## It Started With Sitting in a Restaurant for Hours

Danii started building when he was around 17.

Instead of sitting at home trying to think of the “next billion-dollar startup idea,” he did something much simpler.

He found a real business with a real problem.

A restaurant.

Before building his B2B product, he spent hours inside restaurants observing how they operated.

How did employees work?

Where did they waste time?

What problems did restaurant owners face every day?

What could software actually improve?

He wasn’t building based on assumptions.

He was watching the problem happen in front of him.

That eventually turned into his first B2B application: a table-reservation product for restaurants.

And that first business went on to generate around $40,000.

For a teenager building his first serious product, that alone would have been an incredible story.

But things were only getting started.

## Then Came the Game That Changed Everything

A few years later, Danii entered another chapter of his journey: mobile games.

He built a game connected to the Telegram ecosystem and the events surrounding Telegram founder Pavel Durov.

And then something crazy happened.

The game took off.

It reached roughly 1 million users within a few weeks.

Eventually, Danii exited the project for an amount close to $1 million.

Think about that for a second.

He had gone from a teenager sitting inside restaurants trying to understand their problems…

to having a major exit while still in his early twenties.

And there was another interesting part of his story.

This happened before today’s AI coding boom.

There was no asking an AI agent to build the application.

No one could generate an entire codebase from a prompt.

He had to research, experiment, code, fail, fix things, and learn by himself.

One source of inspiration he mentioned during our conversation was Y Combinator.

He consumed their content and became obsessed with building things people actually wanted.

Eventually, he did exactly that.

But making money and knowing how to handle money are two very different skills.

## Then Everything Started Going in the Other Direction

Imagine getting that kind of money while you’re still extremely young.

Suddenly, life changes.

Danii talked openly about this part of his story.

There was alcohol.

There were expensive things.

There were decisions he wouldn’t necessarily make today.

And slowly, the money started disappearing.

Then came crypto.

Danii was already involved in the crypto world, but this time the market didn’t give him another success story.

He lost a huge part of what he had left.

The person who had once experienced a near-million-dollar exit was suddenly dealing with debt and trying to rebuild his life.

And that’s the part of his story I found much more interesting than the $1 million.

## What Do You Do After Losing Almost Everything?

It’s easy to talk about entrepreneurship when everything is going up.

Revenue screenshots.

User milestones.

Funding announcements.

Successful exits.

But what happens when all of that disappears?

Danii returned home.

His family and friends supported him through a difficult period. He borrowed money and started working again, this time not as the founder of a rapidly growing product, but in a coffee shop.

Today, he works as a barista.

And after his shifts?

He still builds.

Apps.

Experiments.

New ideas.

He is still trying.

That’s what made me want to tell his story.

Because losing money didn’t remove the thing that made him a builder in the first place.

## Building From Russia Added Another Challenge

There was another side of Danii’s journey that we discussed during the podcast: building technology from Russia.

He explained how the war and international restrictions affected parts of the Russian tech ecosystem and made accessing certain global products, payment systems, and business opportunities more difficult.

For someone trying to build internet businesses for a global audience, those restrictions created another layer of difficulty.

Yet Danii continued building.

Not because there was a guarantee that another million-dollar exit was coming.

There wasn’t.

He simply wasn’t finished.

## The $1 Million Isn’t the Most Interesting Part of His Story

When I first heard Danii’s story, the obvious headline was:

“Young founder makes nearly $1 million.”

But after talking to him, I don’t think that’s the most interesting part.

It’s easy to admire someone at the top.

I’m more interested in what they do when they’re back at the bottom.

Danii built a business as a teenager.

He experienced a massive win.

He made mistakes.

He lost a huge amount of money.

He went into debt.

He started working in a coffee shop.

And he kept building.

Maybe his next product succeeds.

Maybe it doesn’t.

But his story is a reminder that being a builder isn’t only about the exit.

Sometimes it’s about opening your laptop again when the previous chapter didn’t end the way you wanted.
